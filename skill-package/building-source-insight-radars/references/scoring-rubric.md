# Scoring and confidence rubric

## Additive score

### Content value · 0–35

- 0–8: promotional, repetitive, or little usable insight.
- 9–17: useful framing but limited novelty or operational depth.
- 18–27: substantial method, evidence, or decision guidance.
- 28–35: unusually original, rigorous, and reusable insight.

### Impact breadth · 0–25

- 0–6: narrow function or isolated use case.
- 7–13: meaningful effect in one function or business unit.
- 14–20: cross-functional operating-model implications.
- 21–25: enterprise, market, regulatory, or ecosystem consequence.

### Target relevance · 0–25

- 0–6: weak transfer to the named audience.
- 7–13: adjacent lesson requiring major adaptation.
- 14–20: clear relevance to active priorities or decisions.
- 21–25: directly changes an imminent high-value decision.

### Evidence strength · 0–15

- 0–3: unsupported assertion or marketing claim.
- 4–7: examples, self-report, or thin/unclear sample.
- 8–11: transparent method and credible multiple observations.
- 12–15: strong primary data, reproducible method, triangulation, or causal design.

`total = content + impact + relevance + evidence`. Store both dimension evidence and the arithmetic total. Do not back-solve dimensions from a total shown by an old radar.

## Priority

Default: `must-read` 90–100, `track` 80–89, `reference` 0–79. Calibrate against the corpus distribution and document any alternate threshold. Tie-break by total, evidence strength, publication date, then stable source order.

## Confidence

Confidence is independent:

- `high`: directly traceable, method/evidence is strong, and transfer assumptions are limited.
- `middle`: credible but contains method, sample, forecast, or transfer uncertainty.
- `low`: thin, inaccessible, unverified, promotional, or heavily inferred.

Explicitly downgrade vendor self-report, forecasts, correlations presented as causation, small or opaque samples, undated claims, transcript-free media, and cross-industry transfer. A high-priority weak signal may still be `low` confidence.
