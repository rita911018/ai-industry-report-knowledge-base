This report highlights the top questions CIOs are currently asking Gartner and provides actionable insights to help you lead through transformation.

## Gartner Answer 1

### Future-proof your enterprise architecture with AI, cloud and edge computing

As business transformation accelerates, CIOs must ensure their enterprise architecture (EA) is agile enough to keep pace with rapid technological change. AI, cloud and edge computing enable CIOs to move beyond traditional, centralized models and deliver business value at speed and scale.

Distributed EA intelligence - a combination of architectural expertise and AI-powered insights - empowers teams to make faster, more informed decisions across increasingly federated and autonomous business units.

CEOs are prioritizing AI investments and expect technology to drive growth. CIOs must rewire EA functions for "AI speeds," which requires a shift from human-mediated guidance to continuous, automated decision support. Architectural intent is enforced by tools, pipelines and AI. Cloud and edge computing further amplify the need for adaptive architectures as decision rights move closer to the edge and centralized control gives way to guardrail-based enablement.

Future-proofing EA is an exercise in measuring how quickly the organization adapts and delivers strategic outcomes. By embracing AI, cloud and edge computing - and redesigning EA operating models for speed, scale and adaptability - CIOs can position EA as a catalyst for innovation and ensure their teams remain indispensable in driving business transformation.

## Gartner Answer 2

### Boost the resilience of your IT operating model

CIOs can build an IT operating model that's more resistant to disruption by diversifying vendors and adopting multicloud/hybrid cloud strategies that reduce reliance on any single provider or region. Flexible, modular architectures supported by automation and edge computing enable rapid adaptation and help maintain operations during disruptions.

Regularly updated disaster recovery plans, strong cybersecurity measures and close collaboration with business and risk leaders ensure critical systems stay protected and aligned with organizational priorities. Investing in workforce agility and remote capabilities further equips the IT team to respond to unexpected challenges.

### Figure: Uncertainty drivers impacting the IT operating model

The operating model centers on performance, supported by decision rights, organizational structures, sourcing and ecosystems, financials, delivery models, talent, tools and platforms, and workplaces. It operates within governance, culture and trust/ethics, under economic, social, technological, environmental, political, and regulatory/legal uncertainty. The organization must execute to strategy across all of these forces.

## Gartner Answer 3

### Modernize legacy systems with a phased, incremental approach

Modernizing legacy systems without disrupting business operations often involves running new and legacy systems in parallel during migration, allowing for thorough testing and gradual cutover to minimize risk and downtime. Prioritizing modernization efforts based on business impact ensures the most critical processes remain stable and supported throughout the transition.

Automation and AI-driven tools can streamline data migration, testing and integration, which further reduces the potential for errors and delays. Close collaboration with business units and transparent communication helps manage expectations, address early concerns and ensure alignment between IT and operational goals. With careful planning and ongoing monitoring, CIOs can achieve modernization while maintaining business continuity.

### Figure: Modernization is continual

Strategy execution and continual scanning feed a continual process. The operating cadence alternates quarterly reviews and event reviews so modernization decisions can respond both to planned checkpoints and new events.

## Gartner Answer 4

### Reorganize human-AI teams, build change reflexes and upgrade capabilities

CIOs must simultaneously tackle team organization, resilience and upskilling to ensure AI transformation succeeds. Begin by adopting a "centaur" collaboration model using cross-functional fusion teams. In this model, AI handles the speed and scale while humans provide context and judgment. To support these new workflows, leaders must develop foundational human capabilities, adaptive change reflexes and AI skills. Transparent communication is critical throughout this process as it prevents change fatigue and quiet resistance.

Businesses cannot pause operations to retrain staff. CIOs must also use a phased, iterative approach to upskilling. Organizations should identify critical skills early and deploy supportive "agent-assist" tools during initial pilots so employees can execute complex tasks and learn. By integrating AI-driven training simulations directly into the flow of work, leaders can continuously upgrade their workforce capabilities without experiencing a disruptive service cliff.

### Figure: Guide workforce evolution from AI tool to AI amplifier

The AI fluency scaffold maps AI fluency against AI engagement. Progression moves from **Tool** to **Toolmate** to **Amplifier**. It develops from the skills people learn, to behaviors they choose, and ultimately to habits that become automatic.
